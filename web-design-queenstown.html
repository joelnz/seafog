Web design in Queenstown.

Where your customer is in Sydney or Singapore, six months out, on a phone, and the booking system matters more than the brochure.

Your customers aren't local

Every other page on this site is about being found by people near you. Queenstown is the exception. Your customer is planning a trip from another country, months ahead, and has probably never set foot in New Zealand.

So the local SEO playbook that works for a Christchurch electrician mostly does not apply. The map pack barely matters. What matters instead is whether your page loads quickly from Sydney or Los Angeles, whether it answers the questions an international visitor has and a local would never ask, and whether the booking goes through on the first attempt on a phone.

If your booking flow loses people halfway through, fixing that is worth more than any amount of SEO.

Booking systems, connected properly

Most Queenstown operators already run a booking platform. The platform is usually fine. The problem is how it's joined to the website: a link that dumps the visitor onto an unbranded page, a widget that breaks on mobile, or an availability calendar three clicks from where anyone looks.

  • Activity and tour platforms such as Rezdy, FareHarbor and Checkfront, embedded so the booking feels like part of your site
  • Accommodation channel managers and booking engines, with the direct booking path made obviously better than the OTA one
  • Availability and pricing surfaced on the page people actually land on, not buried behind a "Book now" button
  • The whole flow tested on a phone, because that is where most of it happens

Every commission you save by moving a booking off an online travel agent and onto your own site goes straight to your margin. That is usually where the return on a Queenstown build comes from, rather than from ranking.

Built for two seasons and an overseas audience

Seasonality

Winter and summer are effectively two different businesses sharing one website. The site should be able to swing between them without a rebuild each April, and without you paying someone to do it.

Loading from offshore

A page that is quick in New Zealand can be slow from Europe. That comes down to image sizes and scripts, and I keep both down when I build it.

Questions locals never ask

Transfers from the airport, what to wear in September, whether it suits children, cancellation in bad weather. These are the pages that earn overseas search traffic.

Proof and reviews

An international visitor cannot ask a neighbour about you. Reviews, certifications and real photographs do that job, and they need to be visible early.

Beyond Queenstown

The same work applies around the lakes and through Central Otago, and those markets are considerably less crowded than Queenstown itself.

Wanaka, Arrowtown, Cromwell, Glenorchy and the Gibbston Valley wineries all run on the same pattern: an overseas customer, a seasonal calendar and a booking system that needs to work. Frankton and Jack's Point businesses serving residents rather than visitors are a different job again, closer to what I do in Wellington, and priced the same way.

Common questions

Do you integrate booking systems?

Yes. Rezdy, FareHarbor, Checkfront and the common accommodation booking engines all embed into Squarespace, though how well depends on the platform. I'll check yours before I quote.

Is local SEO worth it for a Queenstown tourism business?

Less than you would think. Your customers are searching from overseas, so the map pack and Google Business Profile matter far less than they would for a local trade. The exception is businesses serving residents rather than visitors, where the usual local playbook applies. I will tell you which one you are.

Can the site handle winter and summer offerings?

Yes, and it should be built for that from the start. Switching between seasons ought to be something you do yourself in an afternoon, not something you pay for twice a year.

Will the site load quickly for someone booking from overseas?

That is largely decided by how heavy the page is, which is a build decision. I keep image weight and scripts down as standard, and for a Queenstown site I will test the booking flow from an international connection before launch.
